WHERE IT BEGAN

A life among books.
A bridge to a new generation.

Afroz Alam grew up with books and the company of poets whose words seemed to make sense of the world. The ache of Meer, the brilliance of Ghalib, the sharp wit of Zauq—these were not just names on a page. They were voices to return to, lines to remember, and feelings that stayed long after a book was closed.

Then the world changed. For Gen Z, Gen Alpha—and, most personally, for his own children—the world of books and classical poetry could feel far away, while music and digital culture spoke in a language all their own.

Afroz could have asked them to put down their screens and pick up a book. Instead, he asked a different question: what if the poetry could come to them?

What if an old verse could meet a young listener through a sound they already loved? What if a song became the first step towards discovering the poet behind the words? That question became The Sukoon Lab: a way to bring poetry into the sounds of today, without losing the words that made it matter in the first place.

THE IDEA

Don’t ask a generation to go back.
Build a bridge to them.

The aim was never to make younger listeners live like an earlier generation. It was to meet them where they are—and let a song open a door to the poem, the poet and the world behind the words.

The music can evolve. The arrangement can surprise. But the poetry deserves care: its words remain the poets’ own, carried into new listening experiences rather than rewritten to fit a trend.

“Sometimes the way to a book is through a song.”The idea behind The Sukoon Lab
